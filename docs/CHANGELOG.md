# Changelog

Semantic versions start at the first tagged release. This file records what landed before that tag.

## Unreleased

- An owner, or anyone with `members.manage`, can open `/staff/team` to invite someone by email and one of the existing roles. The invitation email uses the same mailer as the other staff messages. The link is signed, expires in 7 days, and is tied to that workspace's verified address. A new person sets a password. Someone who already has an account joins this workspace and keeps that password. Pending invitations can be resent or revoked. A member's role can be changed, and a member can be removed. The last owner cannot be removed or demoted, and nobody can assign a role above their own. Each of those actions is written to the audit log.
- Staff with settings access can manage closures at `/staff/closures`. A closure is a full day or a time range at one location, optionally one category or one boat or pool, with a reason. It can be a single span or the same dates every year. The public booking page and the staff booking and move panels treat that time as unavailable through the existing availability check. Confirmed bookings that overlap are listed with links and are left in place. Nothing is cancelled or refunded. The schedule shades the closed time. Daily reports still count available minutes from opening hours and blackouts only.
- `/staff/manifest` is the day's dispatch sheet. Each location lists its confirmed and no-show bookings in that location's local time, with the assigned resource, customer name and phone, party size and named people, waiver status per person, add-ons, balance due, check-in status, return due time, and staff notes. Held and cancelled bookings stay off the sheet. Print puts one location on each page. The same rows download as CSV. `bookings.view` is required. A member who can edit bookings can save a staff note of up to 1,000 characters. Anonymizing the customer removes their name, email, and phone from that note.
- On `/staff/schedule`, a member who can create bookings can tap an empty hour on a boat or pool. Week view taps the day, then a start time. The New booking panel opens with that resource and start. They choose a service and variant that use that resource, a length, the party size, add-ons, and a customer found by search or entered as new. The quote uses the same direct-channel pricing and booking fee as public checkout. They can hold the boats, record pay at pickup, or send the existing card payment link. A time that is already taken is refused by the allocation locks and is not booked.
- A confirmed booking gets a check-in code on its confirmation page and in the confirmation email. The code is a server-rendered SVG and opens a signed staff link. `/staff/check-in` lists today's arrivals for one location, with search. Opening that link checks the booking in once. It shows the waiver status and the balance due. A missing current waiver blocks check-in unless staff record a reason. Owner, manager, and staff have `bookings.check_in`. The log and array mailers still leave `booking.confirmed` queued.
- On `/staff/schedule`, a member who can edit bookings can tap a block and move that confirmed booking. The panel takes a new start and, for a boat, another active resource in the same category and location. The length stays the same. The move uses the same allocation locks, buffers, and conflict check as a reschedule or reassignment. A conflict leaves the booking where it was and lists the nearby times and same-category resources a refused hold already offers. The change is written to the booking audit trail. The customer reschedule notification is queued. A higher or lower price still uses price increase checkout or the proportional price decrease refund. The move does not charge or refund by itself.
- After a booking is returned or completed, one feedback email is queued. The wait defaults to 2 hours. An owner or manager can set it from 0 minutes through 7 days, and can turn `booking.feedback` off with the other transactional emails. A customer who opts out is skipped, including on a later booking. Opening the opt-out link does not opt anyone out. The log and array mailers record the request and do not send. The email links to a signed page on the workspace's verified domain. The customer rates the visit from 1 to 5 and can leave a comment. The first rating is kept. An optional https review link is shown only after a 4 or 5. `/staff/feedback` lists the ratings, the average, and filters for stars, service, and a comment. The customer export includes the feedback row and the opt-out time. Anonymizing the customer clears the comment and keeps the rating. `feedback:dispatch` runs every five minutes.
- Staff can open `/staff/schedule` for a day or week view of each location and of the boats and pools grouped by category. Bookings and unexpired holds are blocks in that location's local time. Prep and return buffers are shaded. Tapping a block opens it. A member without booking access cannot open the page, and another workspace's rows are not listed.
- A deploy runs `reserveworx:smoke` after the release is current. The command checks the database, migrations, roles, mail, the queue table, the harbor demo booking page, staff sign-in, and signup from inside the application. It prints key=value lines and does not print secrets. A failure fails the deploy and does not roll the release back. A missing scheduler timestamp is reported and does not fail the command.
- A new business can sign up at `/signup` on the platform host. The form asks for the business name, a subdomain, the owner's name, email, and password, and it checks the subdomain as it is typed. Reserved names such as app, www, admin, api, mail, staff, and platform are refused. A honeypot and a rate limit sit on the form. The terms checkbox stores the terms version and the time it was accepted. The workspace is created in onboarding with the standard 3% and 1% booking fees, and the owner gets an owner membership. A verification email has to be used before Go live can make that workspace active. After the link is used, the owner lands on that workspace's `/staff`, which shows a checklist for Stripe, a service and opening hours, branding, a test booking, and Go live. The checklist reads those records. Go live uses the existing status rules and does nothing until the steps are done. Card payments stay off until Stripe can charge the connected account. Platform administrators see the new workspace in the existing list. An owner who is already signed in still adds a business from `/staff/businesses/new`.
- An owner can open `/staff/portfolio` for every business they own, including when they own one. The default is all of those businesses, with totals and a per-business breakdown. A dropdown can limit the totals, the daily trend, and the CSV to one owned business. A workspace id they do not own is not found. A member who is not an owner of the current address cannot open the page. Presets are the last 7, 30, or 90 days, or a custom range. The owner can add a business at `/staff/businesses/new`. Booking fees stay at the standard rates. They can switch to another business they belong to. The session cookie stays on one host, so the switch uses a two-minute single-use token on the other address. `memberships` was already one row per user and workspace, so no membership rows were moved.
- A customer can open an account for one workspace with an email address. The account is optional: a guest can still book without one. The default sign-in is an emailed link that expires in 60 minutes. A password is optional and works only after that link verifies the email. Signing in on the workspace's own host returns a token for that account only. `GET /api/v1/customer/bookings` lists that account's upcoming and past bookings, and `GET /api/v1/customer/receipts` lists their receipts. Guest bookings already stored for the same verified email are linked, including a later guest booking. The customer token cannot call staff routes, and a staff token cannot call these routes. The customer export includes that account and leaves out the password and sign-in token. It includes nothing from the GoHighLevel link: no connection, no sync log, and no private integration token. Anonymizing the last customer row on the account removes the account, its sign-in links, and its tokens. Another customer row that still uses the account keeps the sign-in.
- A platform administrator can link one workspace to one BrandWorx GoHighLevel sub-account. The Location ID and Private Integration Token are stored on that workspace. The token is encrypted and is left out of API responses, logs, audit properties, and the workspace export. Booking created, changed, and cancelled, and a paid payment, queue a contact upsert plus a tag and a note when a link exists. Nothing is queued without a link. `POST /api/v1/platform/tenants/{tenant}/ghl/test` checks the location. Tenant staff cannot call these routes. There is no Marketplace or OAuth app.
- An owner or manager can register an HTTPS webhook for `booking.created`, `booking.cancelled`, `booking.changed`, `payment.succeeded`, and `refund.created`. The signing secret is shown once. Deliveries use the database queue, are signed with HMAC-SHA256, and are retried up to five times. Each attempt records the status, response code, duration, and a truncated body. Private and loopback addresses are refused. Staff cannot register or read them. A delivery from another workspace is not found. This is not a GoHighLevel connection.
- An owner or manager can export one customer and then anonymize that customer. `POST /api/v1/customers/{customer}/exports` queues a JSON zip on the private disk. `GET /api/v1/customers/{customer}/exports/{export}` returns a signed download that expires in 30 minutes. That signed URL is the only download. `POST /api/v1/customers/{customer}/deletion` clears direct identifiers. Booking, payment, refund, and fee rows stay. Signed waiver rows stay because how long they are kept is still open question 3. Deletion is refused until a completed export file exists, unless the request sends `confirm: true`. Repeating it does not change the customer again. Staff cannot call these routes. A customer from another workspace is not found.
- A service can offer variants such as a single kayak, a tandem, or a two-hour rental. Each variant has its own name, duration, optional price, and asset-type mapping. `GET`, `POST`, `PATCH`, and `DELETE /api/v1/services/{service}/variants` use the settings permission. Availability, holds, quotes, and pricing snapshots use the variant when `variant_id` is sent. A price override replaces the open base rate. A peak window still prices the minutes it covers, and the booking fee stays on that service total together with any mandatory operator fee. A service with no variant, or a request that does not name one, is unchanged.
- A workspace can require fees such as cleaning or fuel on a service or a location. A fee is flat, per person, or a percent of the service total before discounts. A hold and the first quote add the lines on their own. Those lines are part of the fee basis and the pricing snapshot, and checkout lists each one. Tax and promos stay on the service total. A party-size change recalculates per-person and percent lines from the snapshot. A flat line stays as quoted. The increase and the decrease include that change together with the retotaled per-person add-ons. A catalog row that is already on a booking cannot be deleted.
- A workspace can store its branding and which transactional emails are on. `GET` and `PATCH /api/v1/branding` save the display name, logo (an https URL or an uploaded PNG, JPEG, WEBP, or GIF), primary and accent colors, and support email and phone. `GET /api/v1/branding/logo` serves that workspace's uploaded file. `GET` and `PATCH /api/v1/notification-settings` save the reply-to address, staff alert recipients, and the on/off switch for each transactional email. Owner and manager can update both. Staff can read them. Existing views receive the current workspace's branding as `workspaceBranding`. No mail is sent, and notification hooks are still queued.
- A price rule can name a local clock window, and optional weekdays, so a service can charge a peak rate. Minutes inside that window use the peak rate in place of the weekday, weekend, or date-bounded rate of the same kind. A booking that crosses the window is the minute-weighted average, rounded half-up. The windows that apply are stored on the pricing snapshot. Spec Section 7.1 does not say how a crossing is split.
- A workspace sets reminder offsets in minutes. The default is 24 hours and 2 hours before the start. `bookings:dispatch-reminders` runs every five minutes and queues `booking.reminder` once for each confirmed booking and offset. Cancelled bookings and bookings that have already started are left out. The local start uses the booking location's timezone. The log mailer records the hook and does not send email. A workspace that turns off the `booking.reminder` email still records that hook and does not send it. Owner and manager read and update the offsets at `GET` and `PATCH /api/v1/booking-reminder-offsets`.
- `POST /api/v1/assets/{asset}/retire` retires one asset. It stays in the workspace, with its past allocations and maintenance records, and it is left out of new availability and allocation. Future bookings that already use it are flagged the same way as a maintenance or blocked asset, and they are not cancelled. An optional replacement must be another active asset in the same category and location. The response offers each future confirmed booking for the existing reassignment route. Accepting an offer runs that reassignment. A conflict leaves the original allocation in place.
- A new hold that is refused because the time is taken keeps its error message and adds `offers` when another time or asset is free. The times are the same service, within two hours of the requested clock time, on that day and the next two days. The assets are other active equipment in the same category and location. A tour offers other departures in that window. The public booking form lists those hints under the error. A refusal for any other reason stays a message only.
- `GET /api/v1/reports/operations` reports cancellation rate, no-show rate, captured card payments, offline payments, customer refunds, taxes, charged booking fees, and booking-fee refunds for a location-local date window of at most 366 days. Rates count confirmed, cancelled, and no-show bookings that start that local day, and stay empty when none of those start. Tax follows confirmed and no-show bookings that start that day. Payments, refunds, and fees follow the day they were recorded in the booking location's timezone. `GET /api/v1/reports/operations/export` downloads the same rows. The read does not write a summary row, and the live today report is unchanged.
- A held or confirmed booking can change party size at `POST /api/v1/bookings/{booking}/party-size`. The change uses the same allocation locks as a reschedule. A conflict keeps the original party and offers a smaller party that fits, plus any other free asset in the same category. The party cannot shrink below people already named on it. A per-person add-on on that booking is retotaled from its stored unit price. Flat and each lines stay. Naming a person does not take payment. A higher price is charged with increase checkout, and a lower price is refunded with the proportional decrease refund. Those paths recompute the fee basis with the chosen add-on lines. Tax stays on the service total.
- A return can record each asset as ok, damaged, or missing, with notes and the staff user who checked it in. `GET` and `POST /api/v1/bookings/{booking}/returns` read and store those rows. The existing return route accepts the same body. A damaged asset is marked in maintenance and future bookings are flagged. The return event queued for GoHighLevel includes the conditions. A late fee is still only a stored amount and is not charged through Stripe.
- A workspace can keep a catalog of add-ons and equipment fees. A booking can choose those lines when it is held or when it is quoted the first time. The line totals are part of the fee basis and the pricing snapshot. Tax stays on the discounted service total. A catalog row that is already on a booking cannot be deleted.
- A booking can name each person in the party. `GET` and `POST /api/v1/bookings/{booking}/participants` list and add them, up to the party size already stored on the booking. A minor stores the guardian's name on that person's record. A waiver signature can point at that person and copies the name, the minor flag, and the guardian. Signing the same waiver again returns the first signature. A person who has signed cannot be removed. The party size used for inventory stays `bookings.participants`.
- `GET /api/v1/reports/tours` lists each tour departure in a date window with the service, the location, the local start time, and the seat cap. Seats booked are confirmed and no-show participants. Seats attended are the confirmed participants. Fill rate and attendance rate are basis points, and stay empty when the cap or the booked seats are zero. Held, expired, and cancelled bookings stay out. `GET /api/v1/reports/tours/export` downloads the same rows. The read does not write a summary row.
- Today's report counts booking-fee refunds recorded on the report date in each booking's location timezone. A refund from another local day stays out of that total. Charged fees and the net fee total stay all-time.
- `GET /api/v1/reports/summaries/export`, `GET /api/v1/reports/revenue/export`, and `GET /api/v1/reports/customers/export` download the same rows as those JSON reports. A blank rate is an empty stored figure. The customer file includes attribution lines. The download does not rebuild a summary.
- `GET /api/v1/reports/customers` counts new and repeat customers for a date window, and counts bookings by the channel stored on the pricing snapshot. A customer is new on the day and location of their first confirmed or no-show booking. Held and cancelled bookings stay out. A booking with no customer is not a customer. The live today report still does not include this.
- `GET /api/v1/reports/revenue` lists gross service cents by service and by the location-local date a booking starts. Confirmed and no-show bookings count. The total for one location on one date matches that day's stored summary gross. The live today report still does not include this breakdown.
- `reports:build-summaries` stores utilization, conversion, average order value, and revenue per resource-hour for each location. `GET /api/v1/reports/summaries` reads those rows for a date window and does not recompute them. The live today report still does not.
- A platform administrator can compare stored booking fees for a UTC date window with the application fees Stripe lists for that same window. The stored fee report still does not call Stripe.
- `GET /api/v1/fee-ledger` lists each booking's fees and the reason each fee was refunded. The workspace totals are unchanged. A booking id from another workspace is not found.
- A deploy opens one SiteGround SSH connection and reuses it for the database backup, the release upload, and activation. It does not scan the host key on a separate connection, and it does not retry a dropped handshake.
- A confirmed booking that moves onto a lower price can be refunded the difference. `POST /api/v1/bookings/{booking}/decrease-refund` returns the subtotal and tax that fell, and a proportional share of the booking fee already charged.
- A platform administrator can refund part of one booking fee with a mandatory reason. The customer is not refunded. The reason is not a Section 7.7 code. The fee report lists that amount separately, and a later customer refund does not return the fee again.
- A confirmed booking that moves onto a higher price can be charged the difference. `POST /api/v1/bookings/{booking}/increase-checkout` adds a booking fee only on that increase. The original fee stays on the first payment.
- A deposit leaves the booking `deposit_paid`. `POST /api/v1/bookings/{booking}/balance-checkout` charges the remaining customer total with no booking fee. The paid webhook marks the booking paid once the captured payments cover that total, and it does not record a second platform fee.
- The public booking confirmation heading wraps a long booking reference so a 390px-wide page does not scroll sideways.
- A workspace can offer Pay at pickup on the public booking review page. The demo shops have it on. That payment is recorded offline and does not include the online booking fee.
- Team sign-in and platform sign-in include a password reset. The emailed link expires in 60 minutes. An unknown or ineligible address gets the same reply as an address that can sign in. A deploy prints whether mail can leave the server and does not send a message.
- A deploy loads the Harbor Demo and Inland Demo sample shops when those workspaces exist, including kayaks, paddleboards, a sunset tour with departures for the next 60 days, prices, and opening hours. It does not change any other workspace. The same deploy adds `michael@brandworx.ai` as an owner of Harbor Demo when that account already exists.

- Staff availability and checkout API calls are rate limited per user and IP. The public booking widget keeps its own limit.
- A push to `main` deploys `app.reserveworx.com` and runs migrations. If `https://app.reserveworx.com/up` is not healthy, the code release is swapped back once. That check does not use localhost.
- The same deploy grants `michael@brandworx.ai` as a platform administrator. When that account is new, its password is written only on the server.
- A deploy writes `index.php` into an existing `harbor.reserveworx.com` document root so it boots the live app, then marks that hostname verified for Harbor Demo. It does not create the site and it does not write under `reserveworx.com`.
- `GET /admin` redirects to `/staff`. The older today page is no longer served.
- An offline payment records the customer total without the booking-fee line. The payment row still stores a fee of zero.
- Staff who can view bookings can list and open customers at `GET /api/v1/customers`. A search matches the stored name, email, or phone. The list is limited to 50.
- Today's report counts confirmed, cancelled, and no-show bookings that start that day, and gives each rate in basis points. Holds and unpaid checkouts stay out of the rate.
- `GET /api/v1/audit-logs` accepts optional `action` and `subject_id` filters. Omitting them still returns the workspace log.
- A `booking.refunded` notification hook includes the booking-fee portion of the customer refund beside the application-fee refund.
- A platform administrator can read stored booking-fee totals by workspace and stored channel at `GET /api/v1/platform/fee-revenue`. The report does not call Stripe.
- Today's report separates gross booking value, tax, booking fees, card payments, offline payments, and customer refunds.
- A signed waiver can be downloaded as a PDF of the stored text, hash, and signature record. That file is not an electronic-signature certificate.
- A platform administrator can export one workspace to a private JSON file. Closing that workspace for good stays refused until the file exists.
- Today's report can be downloaded as CSV from the staff page and from `GET /api/v1/reports/today/export`.
- Staff can refund a Stripe booking. A full refund returns the application fee with Stripe's flag. A partial refund returns the calculator's fee in a separate application-fee refund. A lost dispute does the same. Nothing is sent until the platform secret is set.
